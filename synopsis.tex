\begin{center}
  {\bfseries LectureAssist: An Agentic Multimodal AI Assistant that Turns
   Lectures into Adaptive Study Material}\\[0.3em]
  Fully local question generation from lecture video and course documents,
  with no paid API anywhere in the pipeline
\end{center}

\vspace{0.6em}

\begin{multicols}{2}

%---------------------------------------------------------------
% ABSTRACT | 96 words
%---------------------------------------------------------------
\synsection{ABSTRACT}

Students record their lectures but rarely get the practice questions that
would make those recordings useful. LectureAssist closes that gap. It
ingests a raw lecture video or a course document, classifies every sampled
frame as a slide, a blackboard, a whiteboard or a plain scene, and
preprocesses each class differently before reading it with OCR. Whisper
transcribes the audio in parallel. The two channels fuse into one
timestamped timeline that is summarised, embedded and indexed. A team of
agents then plans topics, drafts questions, checks each one against the
lecture and rewrites whatever fails. Nothing leaves the machine.

%---------------------------------------------------------------
% METHOD | 100 words
%---------------------------------------------------------------
\synsection{Method with System Diagram/Design Complexity}

An orchestrator drives a team of specialist agents. Frames are sampled and
audio demuxed; documents are parsed directly. Brightness and edge
statistics route each frame to its own contrast enhancement, thresholding
and polarity inversion before EasyOCR reads it, while Whisper handles
speech. A planner allocates topic and difficulty slots. A controller then
assigns every slot one cognitive track, so recall, inference and synthesis
questions are enforced rather than hoped for. The generator drafts one
question at a time and may query the FAISS index itself when its context
looks thin. A validator diagnoses failures and a refiner acts on it.

\end{multicols}

%---------------------------------------------------------------
% NOVELTY | 50 words
%---------------------------------------------------------------
\synsection{Novelty of Project}

Two things are genuinely new. Extraction adapts to what it sees instead of
applying one OCR recipe, so chalk on a dark board survives alongside
projected slides. And the validator diagnoses a failure, then picks the
fix: rewrite, fetch more lecture context, or abandon the topic. Comparable
tools only filter.

%---------------------------------------------------------------
% IMPACT | 50 words
%---------------------------------------------------------------
\synsection{Impact on Society/Environment}

Self testing is the strongest way to study, yet practice questions stay a
privilege of rich institutions. LectureAssist generates them from a
student's own lectures on hardware they already own, removing a cost
barrier, not an ability one. A four billion parameter model on one GPU also
spares a datacentre.

%---------------------------------------------------------------
% BUSINESS MODEL | 98 words
%---------------------------------------------------------------
\synsection{Business Model/Feasibility/Financial Scalability Plan}

There is no API bill, so the marginal cost of one more student is
essentially zero. That shapes the plan. The local build stays free and open
source for individuals, reaching students who cannot pay at all.
Departments buy the tier that matters to them: one GPU box serving a whole
course, licensed per seat per semester at a fraction of cloud pricing, with
LMS integration and support as the paid work. Feasibility is not
theoretical. The entire system already runs inside a free Colab session on a
single T4, so one modest GPU can serve a class.

%---------------------------------------------------------------
% CONCLUSION | 50 words
%---------------------------------------------------------------
\synsection{Conclusion}

Every stage of LectureAssist is built and running: ingestion, scene aware
extraction, retrieval, question generation, validation, grading and
adaptive difficulty, all on one consumer GPU. What remains is breadth
rather than depth. Longer multi hour recordings, Bengali lecture audio,
and a classroom trial with real students are the next milestones.
